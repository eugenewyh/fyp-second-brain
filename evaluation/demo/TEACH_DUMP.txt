I care more about a sweet, balanced espresso than a bitter “strong” shot. If the cup tastes harsh, I change grind or dose before I blame the beans.

I prefer medium or light-medium roasted single origins for morning espresso. Dark oily beans taste flat on my machine and clog the basket faster.

My everyday recipe is 18 g in, about 36 g out, in roughly 25–30 seconds. I adjust grind one notch at a time and only change dose if taste stays wrong after three shots.

Milk drinks need microfoam that looks like wet paint, not big bubbles. I steam to about 60–65°C so the latte stays sweet instead of scalded.

I will not buy a cheap blade grinder again. Uneven grounds make sour and bitter in the same cup. A decent burr grinder matters more than upgrading the machine first.

Water matters: very hard tap water leaves scale and mutes flavour. I use filtered water and descale when the flow slows or the steamer sounds rough.

Weekend treat is a flat white with oat milk when I want something lighter. Weekday default is a straight double espresso or a small americano.

Open questions I still have: whether a naked portafilter helps me diagnose channeling, and whether a single-dose grinder is worth it for one person.
